What she walks away with

By the end of camp, she'll have:

Confidence to think for herself and speak with conviction.

Instead of shrinking to fit in or parroting the loudest voice in the group, she learns to trust herself, speak up respectfully, and stand firm in who she is, even under pressure.

Friendships rooted in kindness, boundaries and self-respect.

She learns to build supportive friendships, recognize unhealthy dynamics, and move away from drama, in person and online, without losing herself or her values.

Emotional regulation skills for a pressure-filled world.

Instead of being ruled by big emotions, social stress or comparison, she learns tools to pause, reset and respond thoughtfully, so anxiety stays in check instead of "crashing out."

Leadership from within, not from following the crowd.

Quiet or totally outspoken, she discovers that leadership starts with self-awareness, empathy and integrity, not popularity, peer pressure or performance.

A strong inner foundation that lasts beyond summer.

She leaves grounded in self-worth, confident in her voice, and equipped with tools for school, friends, online and home, long after camp ends.

Why it matters now

Raising a daughter today is fundamentally different than it was a decade ago.

Kids are exposed to more information, more opinions, more content and more pressure, at younger ages than any generation before them. Social dynamics are more public, more permanent and less forgiving.

When a girl doesn't have a strong sense of who she is, the world is quick to step in and define it for her. Those skills don't magically appear later. Girls who don't practice them early often learn the hard way, through missed chances, strained friendships and moments they wish they'd felt brave enough to speak up.

You see it showing up

  • Increased anxiety and emotional swings
  • Difficulty focusing or staying present
  • Friendships that feel fragile, confusing or hard to navigate
  • Attitudes that don't match the values you teach at home

Girls today are asked to

  • Regulate big emotions
  • Manage constant comparison
  • Navigate social media and group dynamics
  • Make decisions in a world full of influence
This isn't because parents aren't doing enough. It's because the environment has changed faster than the tools we're given to handle it.

Every day of camp

How confidence is actually built

The P.O.W.E.R. Framework

Confidence isn't a personality trait. Every day is structured around POWER, a simple, repeatable way girls learn to lead themselves in real life. Not someday. Not when they're older. Now.

  • PPerspective: slow down, challenge unhelpful stories, see clearly.
  • OOwnership: confidence comes from choices, not approval.
  • WWorthiness: "I'm enough," without likes or comparison.
  • EEmpowerment: speak up, set boundaries, take action.
  • RReassurance: recover from mistakes and keep trusting herself.

The Talent Show

Often the most anticipated (and fun) moment of the week. By then friendships have formed and the room feels different, so when girls step on stage they're met with encouragement, not comparison. Some sing. Some dance. Some do comedy. Some surprise themselves by participating at all.

"I showed up, and I did it."

Assigned day one, revealed Friday

Feeling seen, heard, loved and valued

Secret Buddy

On day one each girl fills out an "About Me" page and is thoughtfully paired with a Secret Buddy. All week she practices focusing outside herself:

  • Leaving kind notes and encouraging messages
  • Noticing what makes someone feel special and seen
  • Paying attention, being curious, showing care

Instead of "Do I fit in?" she begins to feel "I matter to someone."

Frequently asked

Camp questions, answered.

Still wondering about something? Email info@girlsmentorship.com

Who is this camp for?

All girls entering 5th through 8th grade, ages 10–13. Whether she's already confident or still finding her voice, camp isn't about "fixing" girls. It celebrates who she is right now while giving her space to grow.

Ages 14–18 can join as a Peer Leader.

What is this camp, and what is it not?
  • A safe space to talk about friendships, confidence and leadership
  • High-energy, where girls get to be kids while building lasting skills
  • A welcoming community where every girl belongs
  • A place to step outside her comfort zone
  • Therapy. We're supportive, but we don't diagnose or treat
  • A political, gender or religious discussion space
  • Passive. Confidence is built through action
  • A guaranteed fix. Growth continues after camp
Can she enroll in more than one week?

Absolutely. Many campers join for two, three or all four weeks. The POWER curriculum stays consistent so she gets more practice; the girls, Peer Leaders and guest speakers make each week feel different. You'll see the option to add weeks at a discounted rate at checkout.

Do you offer a sibling discount?

Yes. During Early Bird, every family automatically gets $100 off each ticket, so a separate sibling discount isn't applied then. After Early Bird, email hello@girlsmentorship.com and we'll share the sibling discount.

When does registration end?

Registration stays open until weeks are full. We've sold out four years in a row, so enrolling early is the best way to secure her spot and the best price. If a week fills we open a waitlist, but spots rarely reopen.

What if my daughter is younger than 10?

Camp is designed for ages 10–13, where the lessons really land. A 9-year-old who's young for her grade or especially mature may still be a great fit. Not sure? Email info@girlsmentorship.com and we'll talk it through.

What is your refund policy?

Before May 1: full refund, minus a $50 processing fee.
May 1 through May 29: 50% refund.
After May 30: no refunds, since her spot is reserved and planning is final.

Can I use ESA funds for camp?

Yes. We're an approved ESA vendor for both camp and Peer Leader enrollment. Email hello@girlsmentorship.com and we'll create an invoice for you to submit through ClassWallet for reimbursement.

Are sponsorships available?

Yes. To sponsor a camper or explore a partnership, email info@girlsmentorship.com or see Sponsor a Girl.
