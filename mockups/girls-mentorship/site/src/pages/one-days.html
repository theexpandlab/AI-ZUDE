A front-row seat to what girls are navigating.

Since 2020, we've worked alongside tween and teen girls through summer camps, after-school programs, school partnerships, workshops, and mentorship. We've listened to their stories, watched them practice these skills in real time, and learned a whole lot about what girls are navigating, and what actually helps.

Our curriculum isn't built around what we think girls need. It's shaped by what we've watched them navigate in real life.

What she walks away with

Imagine her walking away with…

More confidence navigating friendship shifts.

Not because friendships suddenly become easy, but because she has a framework for slowing down, figuring out what's actually happening, and deciding what she wants to do next.

Language for the conversations that feel hard.

Instead of knowing something feels off but having no idea what to say, she's practiced communicating clearly, speaking up for herself, and navigating uncomfortable conversations.

Tools to stop a story before it runs away with her.

One unanswered text can become "she's mad at me," "I did something wrong," or "nobody likes me" pretty freaking fast. She'll practice separating what actually happened from the story she's telling herself.

More clarity around what's hers to own, and what isn't.

Not taking all the blame. Not placing all the blame. Learning to own her choices, her words, her reactions, and what she can do next, without carrying the whole pie.

Practice knowing when to speak up and when to let it go.

Not every uncomfortable moment requires a confrontation, and not every one should be ignored. She'll practice asking: Does this need a conversation? A boundary? A different perspective? Or is this something I can release?

More trust in herself to figure out what comes next.

She doesn't need to have every answer. We want her to start trusting that she has what it takes to find one.

Why now?

We're nearly halfway through the school year. And we're starting to see it.

We work with girls every day, and the same things show up again and again.

Friendships are shifting.

Group chats are complicated.

Comparison is creeping in.

Confidence is taking some hits.

Girls are overthinking what someone said, what they meant, why they weren't included, whether they should speak up… or whether they should just let it go. And we're hearing it from parents, too.

Maybe you're seeing some of it at home. Or maybe she's doing great.

We think both girls deserve the tools.

We don't believe we have to wait until something becomes a problem before we teach girls how to navigate it.

One day can give her something to reach for when real life happens.

No, we're not promising to solve every friendship problem, eliminate comparison, or make middle school magically easy in one day. That's not how this works. But one intentional day can give her:

  • Questions she knows how to ask herself.
  • Language for a conversation she didn't know how to have.
  • A way to separate what happened from what she's assuming it means.
  • Practice speaking up before she has to do it when the stakes feel higher.
  • A framework for deciding what's hers to own.
  • A reminder that someone else's choices don't determine her worth.
  • A little more trust in herself to decide what comes next.

Our goal is for her to walk away thinking

"I have tools. I trust myself. I know what I can do next."

For the girl moms (and dads)

Good questions. Real answers.

Because you're choosing more than a way to fill a Saturday or Sunday.

Does she need to be struggling with friendships or confidence?

No. That's one of the biggest reasons we created ONE-DAYs. If she's doing great, amazing, let's keep building that. We don't think girls should have to wait until they're struggling to build the skills they'll need. And if she's already having a hard time, she's welcome here too.

What if she's coming alone or she's shy?

Please don't let this keep you from sending her! Girls come into Girls Mentorship experiences without knowing anyone all the time. Connection is built into the day through small groups, games, activities, and chances to get comfortable. She does not need to bring a friend to belong here.

Are 5th–8th graders all together?

No. 5th + 6th graders attend Saturday, November 7. 7th + 8th graders attend Sunday, November 8. The foundational skills are similar, but the conversations, examples, scenarios, and depth are adjusted for what girls are actually navigating at each age.

What should she bring?

A packed lunch and a water bottle. We'll provide everything she needs for the day's activities, and we'll have snacks and treats for purchase at the Girls Mentorship Snack Shack. Registered families get final arrival, pickup, and event details in the days before the event.

Where is ONE-DAY held?

Rancho Solano Prep School Upper Campus in Scottsdale, Arizona.

What does the $149 include?

The full six-hour experience, POWER curriculum, activities and materials, small-group practice, Snack Shack, take-home tools, and parent follow-up. Girls bring their own lunch.

Can I use ESA funds for her ONE-DAY?

Absolutely! Girls Mentorship accepts ESA funds. If you need help with the reimbursement process, email hello@girlsmentorship.com and we'll be happy to help.

Does she need to have attended Girls Mentorship before?

Not at all! For returning girls, ONE-DAY is a POWER refresh: a chance to reconnect and get more reps with tools they've already started building. For girls new to Girls Mentorship, it's a perfect introduction to the curriculum.

One day won't remove every hard moment she'll face.

But it can help her feel more equipped for the next one. We don't have to wait until she needs the tools. We can give them to her now.
